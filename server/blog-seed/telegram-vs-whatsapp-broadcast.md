You want to reach your customers in one go. The two obvious choices are Telegram and WhatsApp. They look similar, but for broadcasting they work very differently: different limits, different costs, different rules.

This guide compares **Telegram vs WhatsApp for broadcasting** side by side, in plain words, so you can choose the right one, or use both well.

## The short answer

- **Choose Telegram** if you want to broadcast to a large audience, build a community, automate welcomes and follow-ups, and avoid per-message fees.
- **Choose WhatsApp** if your customers live in WhatsApp, you mostly sell one to one, and your list is small or you are ready for the business platform's costs and rules.
- **Use both** if you can. They are good at different things.

|---|---|---|
| One-to-many feed | Channels, no subscriber limit | Channels (followers cannot reply) |

| Groups | Up to 200,000 members | Up to 1,024 members |

| Buttons | Yes | Yes (templates, interactive messages) |

## How WhatsApp broadcasting works

WhatsApp gives you three ways to reach many people.

### 1. Broadcast lists (WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business app)

- Up to **256 contacts per list**.
- Only people who **saved your number** receive the message.
- It looks like a private message to each person.
- Free.

Great for a small shop with loyal customers. Hard to scale: every list is capped, and "please save my number" is an extra step many people skip.

### 2. WhatsApp Business Platform (API)

This is how larger businesses broadcast on WhatsApp.

- You send **template messages** that Meta must approve first.
- Since 1 July 2025, Meta charges **per delivered template message**. Marketing templates are always billed. Prices depend on the recipient's country.
- New businesses start with a low limit on how many unique people they can message in 24 hours. It rises after business verification and with good quality.
- People must have **opted in** to hear from you.

It is powerful, but you pay for every marketing message, and the setup usually needs a provider.

### 3. WhatsApp Channels

- A one-way feed in the Updates tab.
- Followers can react and vote in polls, but cannot reply.
- No need to save your number.

Good for announcements. Not personal and not interactive.

## How Telegram broadcasting works

### 1. Channels

- **No subscriber limit.**
- Every post reaches everyone in the channel.
- Scheduled and silent posts are built in.
- Comments can be added with a linked discussion group.
- Named invite links show how many people joined through each one.

### 2. Bot broadcasts

- Your bot can message **everyone who pressed Start** in it, privately, with their first name.
- **No per-message fee** from Telegram for normal sending. The limit is speed, about 30 messages per second for bulk sends.
- Buttons, photos, videos, scheduling, segments: all possible with a tool.

How-to: [how to send a broadcast message on Telegram](/blog/telegram-broadcast-message).

### 3. Join-request welcomes

Telegram has something WhatsApp does not: join requests. When someone asks to join your channel, your bot can message them privately for 5 minutes, until the request is handled. So you can welcome every new channel member one to one, and invite them to start your bot. More: [Telegram welcome bot guide](/blog/telegram-welcome-bot).

## Cost: a simple example

Let us say you want to send one promotional message a week to 10,000 people.

| | Telegram | WhatsApp Business Platform |
|---|---|---|
| Channel post | Free, any size | WhatsApp Channel: free, but no replies and not personal |
| Private message to each person | No per-message fee from Telegram. You pay only for your tool, if you use one | 10,000 marketing template messages a week, each billed at your recipients' country rate, plus your provider's fees |

We are not quoting WhatsApp rates here, because they vary by country and change. Check Meta's current rate card for your market and multiply by your list size and frequency. For most businesses broadcasting weekly, the difference adds up fast.

## Automation compared

Broadcasting is only half the job. The other half is what happens automatically when someone new arrives.

| Automation | Telegram | WhatsApp |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome a new follower in private | Yes, for join requests (5-minute window) and for anyone who starts your bot | Business app: greeting message when someone messages you first. Channels: no |
| Timed follow-ups after someone joins | Yes, with a bot tool, to people who pressed Start | Business Platform: template messages, each one billed if marketing |
| Personal first-name messages at scale | Yes, bots | Yes, templates |
| Track which ad brought each person | Start links and named invite links | Click-to-WhatsApp ads report inside Meta |
| Buttons with tracked clicks | Yes, with a tool | Yes, on the Business Platform |
| Approval before you send | No | Business Platform templates need Meta approval |

One thing WhatsApp does very well: **Click-to-WhatsApp ads** inside Meta's ad system open a chat with your business directly, and Meta reports on them natively. If your sales happen in one-to-one chats, that is a real advantage.

One thing Telegram does very well: **automated onboarding for big audiences**. A channel with join requests can greet, filter and follow up with thousands of new members a day without per-message fees.

## How to move part of your WhatsApp audience to Telegram

If you decide to add Telegram, do not spam your WhatsApp contacts with "JOIN MY TELEGRAM" every day. Do it with a reason and with respect.

1. **Give a reason that only exists on Telegram.** A weekly live session, a bigger community, a free resource, early access.
2. **Announce it once or twice** in your WhatsApp Channel or status, with the link.
3. **Use a private invite link with join requests,** so every person who moves over gets a personal welcome.
4. **Track it.** Give the WhatsApp announcement its own named invite link, so you know how many came from there.
5. **Keep WhatsApp for what it does best.** Do not abandon customers who prefer it. Keep support and direct sales there if that is where people buy.

## Reach: where are your customers?

This is the honest counterweight. In many markets, including Nigeria, Kenya and South Africa, WhatsApp is on more phones and used more often day to day. If your customers never open Telegram, the best features in the world will not help.

Ways to decide:

- **Ask your customers.** A quick poll on your other channels.
- **Look at your niche.** Trading, crypto, betting tips, tech, info products and large creator communities are often very active on Telegram. Local shops and services often live on WhatsApp.
- **Test.** Promote both for a month and count real engagement, not just joins.

## Rules and safety

Both platforms punish spam.

- **WhatsApp:** messaging people who did not opt in, or getting lots of blocks, can lower your quality rating and limits, or get your number banned.
- **Telegram:** mass-messaging from a personal account can get it limited. Bots can only message people who started them. Adding people to groups without consent breaks the rules.

On both: no fake investment returns, no misleading claims, and keep trading or betting promotions legal where you and your audience are.

## When to use which

| Goal | Better choice |
|---|---|
| Broadcast to 10,000+ people often | Telegram |
| Build a community with discussion | Telegram (groups up to 200,000) |
| Welcome every new member in private | Telegram (join requests) |
| Automate follow-ups without per-message fees | Telegram |
| One-to-one sales chats with local customers | WhatsApp |
| Customer support where people already chat | WhatsApp |
| Order updates and receipts | WhatsApp Business Platform (utility messages) |

## Five questions to decide

1. **How big is my list, now and in a year?** Under a few hundred, either works. Thousands, Telegram is cheaper to broadcast to.
2. **Do my customers reply and chat, or mostly read?** Chat-heavy favours WhatsApp. Read-heavy favours a Telegram channel.
3. **How often will I broadcast?** Weekly or more, per-message fees on WhatsApp add up.
4. **Do I need automation for new people?** Welcomes and follow-ups at scale are easier on Telegram.
5. **Is my niche sensitive (trading, betting, crypto)?** Both platforms have strict rules. Read them before you build.

## The best of both

A setup we see working well:

1. **Telegram channel** for updates, content and community.
2. **Telegram bot** for welcomes, follow-ups and personal broadcasts.
3. **WhatsApp** for direct sales conversations and support.
4. Link each to the other: "Questions? Chat with us on WhatsApp." "Get daily updates on our Telegram channel."
