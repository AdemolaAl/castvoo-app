Telegram has hundreds of millions of users and some of the most active communities online. For a business, a creator or a marketer, that is a big opportunity, if you know how the pieces fit together.

This is **Telegram marketing for beginners**: what the building blocks are, how they work, and nine simple steps to go from zero to a channel that welcomes, engages and converts. No jargon, no coding.

## Telegram marketing for beginners: the three building blocks

Everything in Telegram marketing is built from three things.

| | Channel | Group | Bot |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | A feed you post to | A chat where members talk | An automated account that sends private messages |
| Who talks | Only admins post | Everyone can talk | The bot talks to each person one to one |
| Size limit | Unlimited subscribers | Up to 200,000 members | Anyone who presses Start |
| Best for | Updates, content, announcements | Community, support, discussion | Welcomes, follow-ups, personal broadcasts |
| Personal? | No, same post for all | Partly | Yes, can use each person's name |

### The one rule to remember

**A bot can only message people who pressed Start in it.** It cannot message strangers. This protects users from spam, and it shapes almost every Telegram marketing strategy.

There is one short exception: when someone asks to join your channel or group, your bot can send them private messages for 5 minutes, until the request is handled ([Telegram Bot API](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api#chatjoinrequest)). That is the window for a welcome.

## Step 1: Pick one clear goal

Before you set anything up, finish this sentence: "People join my Telegram so they can ___, and I want them to ___."

Examples:

- "...get daily football stats, and I want them to join my paid tips group." (Where legal, no guaranteed wins.)
- "...see new stock first, and I want them to order."
- "...learn trading basics, and I want them to take my course." (No profit promises.)
- "...follow my content, and I want them to come to my live sessions."

One goal keeps every post, button and message focused.

## Step 2: Set up your channel properly

- **Name:** what it is, not just your brand. "Lagos Sneaker Drops" beats "LSD Official".
- **Username** (for a public channel): short and easy to type, like `@lagossneakers`.
- **Photo:** clear at a tiny size. A logo or a face.
- **Description:** one line on what people get, one line on how often. "New drops every Friday. Restocks first. No spam."
- **Pinned "start here" post:** what the channel is, when you post, and the one action you want people to take.

### Public or private?

- **Public** channels can be found in Telegram search and joined with one tap.
- **Private** channels use invite links. With "Approve new members" on, each person sends a join request, which lets you welcome them in private.

Many marketers use both: a public username for discovery, and a private invite link with join requests for ads and partners.

## Step 3: Create a bot

1. Open **@BotFather** in Telegram.
2. Send `/newbot`.
3. Choose a display name (like "Ada from Lagos Sneaker Drops") and a username ending in "bot".
4. Copy the token BotFather gives you. Keep it secret; it controls your bot.
5. Set a photo (`/setuserpic`) and a short description (`/setdescription`), so the bot looks trustworthy.

## Step 4: Welcome every new member

The first minute after someone joins is when they are most interested. A good welcome:

- Uses their **first name**.
- Says in one line **what they get**.
- Gives **one next step**, ideally a button to start your bot, with a real reason (a guide, a discount, a schedule).

For channels, this works through join requests. Your bot needs to be an admin with the right to add members, and your invite link needs "Approve new members" on. Then your bot welcomes each person and lets them in automatically.

## Step 5: Plan your content

You do not need to post all day. You need to be **reliable** and **useful**.

A simple weekly plan for a beginner:

| Day | Post type |
|---|---|
| Monday | A useful tip or how-to |
| Tuesday | News or an update in your niche, explained simply |
| Wednesday | Behind the scenes, or a question to your audience (polls work well) |
| Thursday | Your product, offer or service, with one button |
| Friday | A recap, a resource, or a story |

Tips:

- **Short posts win** on phones. If it needs a long scroll, split it.
- **Make posts forwardable.** Useful, clear, one screen. Forwards bring free members.
- **Schedule ahead.** Hold the send button and pick "Schedule message".
- **Use silent posts** for late-night or low-priority updates.

## Step 6: Grow with real people

The fastest beginner wins:

1. Add your channel link to every bio and your website.
2. Tell your existing customers or followers, with a reason to join today.
3. Swap shout-outs with channels of a similar size and topic.
4. Post things people want to forward.

**Never buy members.** They are fake or inactive, they make your channel look dead, and adding people without consent breaks Telegram's rules.

Full guide: [how to grow a Telegram channel without fake members](/blog/grow-telegram-channel).

## Step 7: Build your bot subscriber list

Your channel reaches everyone, but always as a crowd. Your bot reaches each person privately. The more of your audience presses Start in your bot, the more you can do: personal messages, follow-ups, reminders, segments.

Ways to get Start taps:

- A "Tap to start" button in your welcome.
- A free resource only the bot delivers.
- Your bot link (`t.me/yourbot`) in your bio and posts.
- "Remind me" buttons before live sessions.

## Step 8: Follow up and broadcast

Now you have two tools:

- **Follow-ups (drips):** messages that send themselves to each new person at set times. Welcome, then a useful message a few hours later, then your offer a couple of days later. See [Telegram drip campaigns](/blog/telegram-drip-campaigns).
- **Broadcasts:** one message to many people at a time you choose, for news, launches and live sessions. See [how to send a broadcast message on Telegram](/blog/telegram-broadcast-message).

Always include an easy way to stop receiving bot messages. People who cannot stop politely will block you.

## Step 9: Measure what matters

Skip vanity numbers. Watch these:

| Number | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| New members per week | Is growth happening? |
| Views per post ÷ members | Are people actually reading? |
| Bot subscribers ÷ members | How much of your audience can you reach privately? |
| Button clicks | Are people taking action? |
| Leaves and blocks after a post | Did a post annoy people? |
| Members by source | Which promotion works? |

To see which source brings which people, use a different invite link or bot start link per source. Guide: [how to track which ad brought each Telegram subscriber](/blog/track-telegram-subscribers-from-ads).

One limit to know: Telegram does not give bots read receipts. No tool can show you who read a message. Clicks are the reliable signal.

## 6 beginner mistakes to avoid

1. **Starting with ads before the basics work.** Paying to send people to a channel with no welcome and no pinned post wastes money. Set up steps 1 to 5 first.
2. **Posting in bursts.** Ten posts on Monday, nothing until Friday. People mute channels that flood them. Steady beats busy.
3. **No reason to press Start.** "Start my bot" is not a reason. "Get the free price list" is.
4. **Copying big channels' tone.** You are not a news agency. Sound like a helpful person.
5. **Ignoring where members come from.** Without a separate link per source, you cannot tell what is working.
6. **Selling in every post.** A good rough balance is mostly useful posts with an offer now and then. If every post sells, people stop reading.

## The rules, in plain words

- Message only people who started your bot or joined your channel or group.
- No bought lists, no scraping, no adding people without consent.
- No scams, fake investment returns, misleading claims or impersonation.
- Trading, betting and crypto promotions must be legal where you and your audience are, and must never promise profits.
- Follow Telegram's Terms of Service.

## Mini glossary

- **Channel:** a one-way feed. Admins post; subscribers read.
- **Group:** a chat where members can talk.
- **Bot:** an automated account that sends messages and buttons.
- **BotFather:** Telegram's official bot for creating bots.
- **Join request:** a request to join a channel or group when the invite link needs approval.
- **Start link:** a bot link with a label, like `t.me/yourbot?start=instagram`, showing where someone came from.
- **Broadcast:** one message to many people.
- **Drip / follow-up:** messages that send automatically at set times after someone joins or starts your bot.
