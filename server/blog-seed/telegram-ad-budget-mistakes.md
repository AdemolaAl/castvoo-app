Media buyers often ask me why their Telegram campaigns "don't work". Very often, the ads are fine. The money is leaking somewhere between the click and the conversation.

Here are the seven mistakes that waste the most **Telegram ad budget**, in the order I check them, and the fix for each. None of them need a bigger budget to fix. Most need ten minutes and a bit of discipline.

## Why most Telegram ad budget is lost after the click

Ads Manager shows you the cost of a click. It does not show you what happened next. On Telegram, "next" has several steps: the app opens, the person requests to join, gets in, and either hears from you or does not. Every mistake below lives somewhere in those steps.

## Mistake 1: Leaving join requests in a queue

You turned on "Approve new members" because someone told you it filters spam. Good. Then nobody approves the requests for six hours.

The person who tapped your ad at 9pm wanted in at 9pm. By 3am they have forgotten you. You paid for that click.

**How to spot it:** open your channel's member requests. If there is a pile there, you are paying for people who are waiting outside the door.

**Fix:** auto-approve. A bot that is an admin with the right to add members can approve every request in seconds. Step-by-step: [how to auto-approve Telegram join requests](/blog/auto-approve-telegram-join-requests).

## Mistake 2: Silence after the join

The request is approved. They are in. And then... nothing. They see a channel feed full of old posts and no idea where to start.

Worse, you just missed the only moment your bot could talk to them in private. When someone asks to join, Telegram gives your bot 5 minutes to message them, until the request is handled ([Bot API](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api#chatjoinrequest)). After that, you can only message them privately if they press Start in your bot.

**How to spot it:** join your own channel from a second account using your ad link. Count the seconds until you hear from the channel. If the answer is "never", this is your leak.

**Fix:** welcome first, approve second. Short message, their first name, one button to start your bot with a real reason to tap.

## Mistake 3: One link for every ad

Five ads, three countries, two audiences, one link. The campaign gets 2,000 joins. Which ad worked? No idea. So you scale all of them, including the two that bring people who never read a post.

**How to spot it:** ask yourself which ad brought your last 100 bot subscribers. If you cannot answer in one minute, you have this problem.

**Fix:** a start link (`t.me/yourbot?start=fb_ng_v2`) or a named invite link for every ad. Same name as the ad in Ads Manager. Setup and naming system: [how to track which ad brought each Telegram subscriber](/blog/track-telegram-subscribers-from-ads).

## Mistake 4: Worshipping cheap clicks

Ad A gets joins at half the price of Ad B. You kill B and scale A.

Then you look closer. Ad A's people almost never press Start, never click a button, block the bot quickly. Ad B's people do all of those things. You just scaled the worse ad.

**How to spot it:** you have ever paused an ad only because its cost per click was higher than the others.

**Fix:** judge ads on **cost per banked lead** (spend ÷ people who pressed Start) and their value over 30 days, not cost per click or per join. I explain the whole framework in [Traffic Banking](/blog/traffic-banking).

| Metric | What it tells you | Trust it for scaling? |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per click | Ad appeal | No, on its own |
| Cost per join | Price at the door | Partly |
| Bank rate | Quality of the people | Yes |
| Cost per banked lead | Real price of a reachable contact | Yes |
| 30-day value per banked lead | Whether the business works | Yes, the final word |

## Mistake 5: Renting your audience instead of owning it

A channel member is rented. You can reach them only as one face in the feed, and only until they mute or leave. A bot subscriber is owned: you can message them in private, by name, when it matters.

Many buyers build 50,000-member channels with almost no bot subscribers. When engagement drops, they have no direct line to anyone.

**How to spot it:** divide your bot subscribers by your channel members. If it is a tiny fraction, most of your audience is rented.

**Fix:** make the Start tap the main goal of your welcome. Offer something worth tapping for: a checklist, a guide, a schedule, a bonus that is real. Use a "Tap to join" mode for traffic sources that bring low-quality accounts, so people are only let in after pressing Start.

## Mistake 6: Selling to strangers in message one

The welcome says "Join VIP now, 70% off, ends tonight!" to someone who joined 4 seconds ago. They leave.

People need a reason to trust you before they buy anything. That takes a few touches.

**How to spot it:** read your welcome out loud. If the first thing a stranger hears is a price, a discount or "VIP", you are selling too early.

**Fix:** a short follow-up sequence. Welcome and value first, offer on Day 2 or 3, answer objections after. Example sequences: [Telegram drip campaigns](/blog/telegram-drip-campaigns).

## Mistake 7: Breaking the rules to get cheaper results

"Guaranteed 300% returns." "Never lose a bet again." Fake news-style creatives. Cloaked landing pages.

It works until it does not. Then you lose the ad account, the business manager, sometimes the payment method, and the pixel history you spent months building. In trading, betting and crypto, misleading claims also hurt real people.

**How to spot it:** search your ads, landing page and welcome for words like "guaranteed", "sure", "risk-free", "daily profit" or "never lose". Every hit is a risk to your account.

**Fix:** read Meta's [Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/) for your category and countries. Online gambling needs Meta's prior written permission. Crypto and many financial products are restricted. Say what you teach or share, not what people will earn. Make it legal where you and your audience are.

## What a healthy Telegram ad budget looks like

Here is the shape I want to see when I review a campaign. The numbers are a made-up example to show the pattern, not a benchmark. Your market and niche will be different.

| Step | Unhealthy funnel | Healthy funnel |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks | 2,000 | 2,000 |
| Joins | 900 | 800 |
| Request approved within a minute | 40% | 100% |
| Got a private welcome | 0% | 98% |
| Pressed Start (banked) | 5% of joins | 45% of joins |
| Banked leads | 45 | 360 |
| Got a follow-up on Day 1 to 3 | 0 | 360 |

Same clicks. Same spend. The healthy funnel has eight times more people it can actually talk to. That difference has nothing to do with the ad. It is all after the click.

The pattern to remember: **fix the funnel before you fix the ads.** A better creative pushes more people into a leaky bucket. A fixed bucket makes every creative worth more.

## How to split your Telegram ad budget

A simple split I like for anyone starting out:

- **70% on proven ads** (your best cost per banked lead from last week).
- **20% on new creatives** against the same audience.
- **10% on new audiences or placements.**

Review weekly, by tag. Move money from the bottom of your list to the top. Never move money based on cost per click alone.

## The 10-minute budget audit

Run through this before you spend another dollar.

| Check | Yes / No |
|---|---|
| Is every join request approved within seconds? | |
| Does every new joiner get a private welcome before approval? | |
| Does the welcome have one clear "Tap to start" reason? | |
| Does every ad have its own start link or invite link? | |
| Do ad names match the link names? | |
| Do I know my cost per banked lead per ad? | |
| Is there a follow-up for at least 3 days? | |
| Do broadcasts have an easy stop button? | |
| Have I read the ad policy for my category this month? | |
| Am I free of profit promises in ads, landing pages and messages? | |

Every "No" is money leaking. Fix the first one on the list before you touch your bids or creatives.
