I run paid traffic on Meta across Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, South Africa and Cameroon, and I have won awards and media buying contests along the way. The biggest lesson is not about creatives or bids. It is this: **most Telegram budgets do not die in the ad account. They die in the gap between the click and the conversation.**

This is my playbook for running **Facebook ads to a Telegram channel**: compliant, measurable, and built so that what you pay for stays paid for.

No profit promises here. Paid traffic is a skill with real risk. What I can give you is a structure that stops the obvious waste.

## Step 0: Compliance before creatives

I put this first because getting banned is the most expensive mistake in media buying. Your ad account, your business manager and sometimes your payment method go with it.

Read Meta's [Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/) for your category. The ones Telegram buyers hit most:

| Category | What to know |
|---|---|
| Online gambling and games | Needs Meta's prior written permission, must follow local law, and must target legal ages |
| Cryptocurrency | Restricted; eligibility depends on licences and country |
| Financial and insurance products | Restricted; some products are prohibited, others need authorisation or disclaimers |
| Misleading claims | No guaranteed returns, no "get rich" promises, no fake before-and-after |
| Personal attributes | Do not imply you know the viewer's finances or situation ("Broke? Click here") |
| Cloaking | Showing reviewers a different page from users is banned. Do not do it. |

Rules change. Check the current version for each country you target, every time.

[[note]]
**Simple test for your copy:** would you be comfortable reading this ad out loud to the person's mother? If it promises money, hides risk or pretends to be news, rewrite it. In trading, betting and crypto especially, say what you teach or share, never what people will earn.
[[/note]]

## Why Telegram is a strong destination

- **You own a channel to reach people again.** A landing page gets one visit. A channel or bot gets many.
- **Private messages get attention.** A bot DM lands where people talk to friends.
- **Join requests give you a moment with every person.** More on this below.

## The leak: where paid joins disappear

Every Telegram funnel has the same steps. Each one loses people.

1. Sees the ad
2. Clicks
3. Landing page loads (if you use one)
4. Taps the Telegram link
5. Telegram opens (or does not, if the app is not installed)
6. Requests to join or joins
7. Gets let in
8. Presses Start in your bot (**banked**)
9. Engages over the next days

Most buyers measure steps 1 to 2 in Meta, glance at step 6 in Telegram, and never look at 8 and 9. That is like a shop counting people at the door and never checking the till.

## Three ways to run Facebook ads to a Telegram channel

### Setup A: Ad → landing page → channel (join requests)

The landing page explains the channel, carries your pixel, and links to a private invite link with "Approve new members" on.

- **Pros:** pixel works on the landing page; you can explain the offer and the risks properly; join requests give you a welcome moment.
- **Cons:** one extra step.
- **Best for:** most channels, and anything in a sensitive category, because the landing page is where you put clear disclaimers.

### Setup B: Ad → bot start link

The ad sends people straight to `t.me/yourbot?start=ad_tag`. They press Start and are banked at once.

- **Pros:** shortest path to a banked lead; the start tag records the ad.
- **Cons:** no pixel on a page; the bot must do all the explaining.
- **Best for:** lead magnets ("tap to get the free guide").

### Setup C: Ad → landing page → bot → channel

The landing page links to the bot with a start tag. The bot welcomes, delivers value, then invites them into the channel.

- **Pros:** banked first, channel second; full source tracking.
- **Cons:** more pieces to build.
- **Best for:** buyers who want every lead banked and attributed.

## The landing page checklist

If you use Setup A or C, your landing page does three jobs: it passes Meta's review, it carries your pixel, and it makes the Telegram tap feel like the obvious next step. Mine always has:

- **One headline that matches the ad.** If the ad says "free beginner checklist", the page says the same words. Mismatch kills trust and conversion.
- **What the channel is, in two lines.** What you post, how often, who it is for.
- **Who it is not for.** In sensitive niches this line protects you and filters bad traffic.
- **Clear risk wording** where it applies. Trading and betting carry risk of loss. Say it plainly.
- **One button.** "Join on Telegram". Not three.
- **A note for people without Telegram.** "Don't have Telegram? It's free on Play Store and App Store." A share of your mobile clicks will not have it installed.
- **Fast load on a cheap phone and slow data.** In many of my markets, a heavy page burns half your clicks before it even loads. Keep it light.
- **Privacy policy and contact details** in the footer.

## Creative angles that work without breaking rules

You do not need hype to get clicks. You need a clear reason to join. Angles I like:

| Angle | Example (adapt to your niche) |
|---|---|
| The free resource | "The 1-page checklist we give every new member. Free in our Telegram." |
| The schedule | "Market notes every weekday at 8am WAT. Join the channel." |
| The community | "4 live Q&A sessions a month. Ask anything about getting started." |
| The behind the scenes | "How we test products before we post them." |
| The honest filter | "Not for people looking for get-rich-quick. For people who want to learn." |

Notice what is missing: income claims, screenshots of profits, fake urgency. Those get ads rejected and attract the people who will block your bot first.

## Tracking: one link per ad

Meta cannot see inside Telegram. So you need your own labels.

- **For the bot:** a start link per ad, like `t.me/yourbot?start=fb_ng_vid3`. Telegram allows up to 64 characters of letters, numbers, `_` and `-` in that tag.
- **For the channel:** a named invite link per ad. Telegram counts joins per link.

Name tags so you can read them at a glance: platform, country, creative. Example: `fb_ke_carousel2`.

If you want Meta to optimise on Telegram events (joins, bot starts) instead of landing page views, you need a tool that sends those events to Meta's Conversions API. TG Tracker is built for that. ## Campaign structure that keeps you sane

- **Objective:** with Setup A or C, optimise for an event your pixel can see on the landing page. With Setup B, you will likely run a traffic objective and judge results by start tags.
- **One variable per test.** Test creatives in one ad set, audiences in another. Not both at once.
- **Name everything the same way** as your tags, so Meta reports and Telegram data line up.
- **Give tests a fair budget** before judging. Killing an ad after 20 clicks is guessing.

## Stop the leak: the welcome and follow-up

This is where I see the most money saved, and it costs almost nothing.

### Welcome inside 5 minutes, before approval

When someone requests to join, Telegram gives your bot 5 minutes to message them, until the request is handled ([Bot API](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api#chatjoinrequest)). So:

1. Send the welcome at once, with their first name.
2. Include a "Tap to start" button with a real reason to tap.
3. Approve them after the welcome is sent.

### Follow up for 5 days

| Day | Message |
|---|---|
| 0 | Welcome + "Tap to start" for the free resource |
| 0 (+2 to 3 hours) | Deliver it. One line on what the channel is for. |
| 1 | Education: how it works, the risks, who it is not for |
| 2 | Your offer, one tracked button |
| 4 | Answer the top objection (only to people who did not click) |

More sequence ideas: [Telegram drip campaigns](/blog/telegram-drip-campaigns).

### Filter junk traffic

If an ad brings lots of fake or low-quality accounts, use a "Tap to join" step: people are let in only after they press Start in your bot. Your bank rate becomes your quality score.

## The numbers I judge every ad on

| Metric | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| CPM, CTR, CPC | Meta Ads Manager |
| Landing page to Telegram tap rate | Your landing page analytics |
| Joins per ad | Named invite links, or start tags |
| Bank rate (Start taps ÷ joins) | Your bot tool |
| Cost per banked lead | Spend ÷ banked leads, per ad |
| 30-day value per banked lead | Your sales or offer data, by tag |

**Scale** ads with a good cost per banked lead and healthy 30-day value. **Kill** ads with cheap clicks but a poor bank rate. Cheap clicks that never bank are the most expensive traffic you will ever buy.
